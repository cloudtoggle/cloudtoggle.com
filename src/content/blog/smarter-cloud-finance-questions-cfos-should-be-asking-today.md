When cloud adoption surges, controlling spend becomes crucial. According to **Forrester**, public cloud revenue is expected to more than double, growing from **$446 billion in 2022 to $1.03 trillion in 2026** ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/report/the-public-cloud-market-outlook-2022-to-2026/RES178311?utm_source=chatgpt.com)). While this represents tremendous opportunity, it also raises challenges for finance leaders: how to empower innovation while ensuring cost-efficiency?

### The CFO’s Cloud Finance Challenge

Finance teams must balance cost discipline with agility. Public clouds offer tremendous flexibility, but with that comes complexity in forecasting and optimization. Without tight alignment between Finance and IT, cloud spending can quickly spiral out of control.

Smart CFOs ask the right questions to keep cloud costs in check:

* * *

### 1\. Are we treating cloud like OpEx, not CapEx?

Many organizations bring legacy purchasing behaviors into the cloud, over-provisioning in the belief that more capacity equals more flexibility. The reality: leased cloud compute can be dialed up and down. CFOs must insist on an **OpEx mindset**, where resources should be paid for only when needed and not pre-allocated.

![](/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/stockvault-cloud-technology-executives-with-head-on-digital-cloud266889-300x180.jpg)

* * *

### 2\. Are idle resources wasting our budget?

The cloud runs 24/7, but many workloads, especially non-production environments, are active only during business hours. If you’re paying for VM uptime simply because the clock doesn’t stop, you’re overspending. And it’s not just idle time – resources are often oversized: one platform noticed average CPU utilization of just **4.9%**, hinting at 50–70% potential savings through downsizing.

* * *

### 3\. Do we have automation in place to control spend?

Growth-minded IT teams prioritize innovation, but finance must ensure there’s a cost control framework baked in. That means setting up automated, auditable scheduling of workloads containing start/stop rules, custom overrides, and clear cost reporting; team by team, project by project.
